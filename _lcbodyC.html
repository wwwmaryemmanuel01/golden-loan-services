What the figures assume
Read this before quoting a customer
RuleHow it works here
Flat interestInterest is charged on the original principal for the whole term, so every instalment is the same size.
Reducing balanceInterest is charged on what is still owed each period, so the interest falls as the loan is paid down.
Equal instalmentsOne steady instalment throughout; a larger share of principal is repaid each period and the interest falls.
Equal principalThe principal is repaid in equal slices and the interest is added on the reducing balance, so instalments fall each period.
Processing feeCharged once as a percentage of the principal and deducted from the cash handed over, the way the book charges it. It is a cost of the loan and is not repaid again, so total repayment is the principal plus interest.
Rate basisThe rate is quoted per month. Weekly and fortnightly loans convert it from the annualised rate so the cost of borrowing does not change with the payment rhythm.
Annual periodThe number of months the rate is annualised over; the default is a 1-year period, i.e. 12 monthly periods.
DatesThe first instalment falls one period after the disbursement date; month ends are clamped so a loan on the 31st never rolls into the next month.
RoundingEvery figure is rounded to the nearest kwacha; the last instalment absorbs the difference so the balance closes at exactly zero.
Not includedLate payment penalties, insurance, valuation, stamp duty and any negotiated discount. Those are added by Fees & Penalties and the loan agreement.
